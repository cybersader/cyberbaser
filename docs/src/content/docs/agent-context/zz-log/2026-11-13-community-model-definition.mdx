## Direction

After requiring both short- and long-term architecture, the maintainer explicitly asked to define the model intuitively and crystallize it in the knowledge base. This authorizes a coherent product definition, not implementation or automatic selection of every proposed permission rule.

The existing [contribution, trust and access page](/cyberbaser/design/contribution-trust-and-access/) was rewritten in place as **Community contribution and governance model**. Its path and long-term architecture anchor remain stable. No parallel competing model page was created.

## Definition

The opening model is: a reader can help without joining; a contributor can build a history; a trusted contributor can earn more freedom; a steward can receive a specific responsibility; the owner controls policy and source.

Four everyday journeys make that concrete: a signed-out correction, returning contribution, recognized expertise and delegated care of a collection. Each distinguishes the intended experience from implementation evidence. Anonymous status/reply mechanics, rich revisions and discussion, earned-trust progression and general delegated stewardship are explicitly unbuilt or unselected rather than invented as current features.

Local familiarity (new, known, trusted) is an explanatory axis, not a selected numbered trust schema. Review, Apply, Publish and administration are separate responsibilities, not automatic promotion levels. Proposed grants use a human-readable sentence: this person may do this action on this content under these conditions until the grant ends, with a destination for publication. An account/passkey does not supply that grant.

The definition also covers revision-bound decisions, current source/permission checks, revocation, private evidence visibility, anonymous contribution, credit, source-qualified identity, proposal lifecycle versus job outcomes, and the distinction between selected publication, private collaboration and cross-base sharing. Near-term owner-only effect authority is one configuration of the target, not a permanent single-person assumption.
