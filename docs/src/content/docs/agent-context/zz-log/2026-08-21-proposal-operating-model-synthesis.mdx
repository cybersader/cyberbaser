## Why this wave happened

Two owner-review implementations passed their mechanical checks but failed the maintainer's product test. The first looked like an engineering evidence dashboard. The second reduced evidence density but still made Current versus Proposed and the required action insufficiently obvious. Its headings were derived from changed words, which could not represent realistic paragraph, section, page, or multi-operation work. The maintainer also could not place intake, queue, private review, immutable decision, later application, Git, rendering, and deployment in one intuitive system model.

The maintainer explicitly rejected building title fields, taxonomy, operation arrays, mockups, or deployment diagrams as isolated scaffolding. They requested an organized dynamic ultracode workflow grounded in the complete canonical Cyberbaser knowledge base and accumulated vision.

## Workflow shape and execution

A task-specific workflow was authored with a literal hard cap of 11 agents and launcher-model inheritance. It contained five phases:

1. four independent context audits covering product direction, exact-change mechanics, authoring/governance vocabulary, and deployment reality;
2. one reconciled working brief;
3. four coordinated designs covering proposal intent, exact change sets, editorial workflow/taxonomy, and UX/fixtures/deployment;
4. one adversarial critic; and
5. one final synthesizer.

The first nine agents completed successfully. The adversarial critic lost the API stream before returning:

```text
stream disconnected before completion: stream closed before response.completed
```

The workflow therefore stopped before the final synthesizer. A guarded resume was attempted once and refused because the remaining agent cost could not be enforced against the original reservation. The guard was not retried or weakened.

The completed agent work consumed approximately 1.35 million subagent tokens. This substantially exceeded the initial 180,000–320,000 estimate. The estimate was wrong and is recorded here rather than hidden. After the maintainer explicitly authorized a changed execution path, the main session performed the adversarial review and synthesis directly from the nine completed structured results.

No delegated agent modified repository files or spawned sub-agents.

## Context findings

The audits converged on these facts:

- Cyberbaser remains an interoperability layer for contributable, version-controlled knowledge bases.
- The current v1 product is the owner-controlled change boundary around one canonical Markdown corpus.
- External spokes propose only. Owner-local direct authoring remains a distinct authority lane.
- Proposal v1 is one existing Markdown file plus one exact quote- or offset-bound splice.
- The queue retains proposal evidence only and keeps `pending-review` or `expired` lifecycle.
- Owner approval is immutable decision-only intent. It performs no source, Git, rebuild, deployment, publication, queue-lifecycle, or owner-job effect.
- The later application boundary is selected conceptually but lacks an authority event, input contract, and implementation.
- The review implementation's changed-byte-derived headings and typo-only fixtures are structural product defects, not merely styling defects.
- The same-host review proof is local process evidence. Tracked OCI examples keep review disabled because shared-socket mounting and cross-container authorization are unimplemented.
- Synthetic, maintainer, independent-human, and live-effect evidence remain separate.

## Adversarial reconciliation

The four design workstreams agreed on the broad shape but disagreed in important details. The main-session critique applied these corrections.

### Title and summary

One design required a title. Another correctly warned that a core title requirement would encourage adapters to invent contributor wording. The synthesis recommends:

- summary required for newly created richer proposals;
- title optional at the core contract level;
- native forms request a title and may require contributor confirmation;
- adapter suggestions remain separate review evidence unless explicitly adopted before submission; and
- all display fallbacks expose provenance and never derive durable identity from changed bytes.

### Taxonomy

One design proposed nine multi-select categories. Another proposed six broad required categories plus local tags. Neither is locked. The recommended fixture pilot uses one broad primary category (`correction`, `clarification`, `expansion`, `restructure`, `maintenance`, or `other`) plus optional facets such as sourcing, link repair, metadata, navigation, or new content.

Categories remain untrusted claims. They may add requirements or increase review but cannot reduce scrutiny, grant trust, approve, or authorize application.

### Exact operation scope

The broad designs included multi-operation, multi-file, and new-file cases. Combining all three would make the first version responsible for queue accounting, path authority, transactions, locking, rollback, and recovery at once.

The recommended first executable successor is narrower:

- one repository;
- one immutable base revision;
- one existing Markdown file;
- multiple base-relative non-overlapping operations;
- one candidate built in a single left-to-right pass; and
- one atomic owner decision.

Multi-file and new-file examples remain conceptual fixtures until their authority and recovery models exist.

### Workflow state

The taxonomy design risked specifying a workflow engine. The synthesis keeps most conditions as independently derived evidence:

- queue lifecycle;
- exact binding;
- content fidelity;
- trust route;
- review attention;
- owner decision;
- application authority;
- application result;
- deployment observation; and
- publication witness.

A single generic status is rejected. The exact application-authorization artifact, expiry, revocation, and durable-policy semantics remain open. Only the separation is locked: approval alone never authorizes application.

### UX and deployment

The recommended review pattern is:

- authored proposal identity and summary;
- mechanically computed actual scope;
- simultaneous Current and Proposed columns on wide screens;
- operation grouping and navigation for longer work;
- a persistent decision rail with a required note and deliberate confirmation;
- stacked Current then Proposed evidence on mobile;
- exact content evidence near each comparison;
- technical identifiers behind disclosure; and
- receipts led by the immutable decision and **Source unchanged**.

The first supported review profile remains same-host local processes. Portagenty is a development/review launcher, not deployment. OCI review stays disabled until shared-socket authorization and restart evidence pass. Public Lane B exposure remains a separate ingress, abuse, deployment, and human-evidence decision.

## Canonical result

The full synthesis is now recorded in [Proposal operating model](/cyberbaser/research/proposal-operating-model/). It defines:

- the three-layer product object;
- intent authorship and provenance rules;
- the first exact multi-operation successor;
- separate operating axes and authority boundaries;
- a candidate editorial taxonomy;
- side-by-side and mobile review requirements;
- the same-host and future OCI deployment model;
- a realistic executable and conceptual fixture matrix;
- the adversarial constraints;
- the phased sequence to implementation; and
- unresolved maintainer and architecture decisions.

This is a working architecture recommendation, not a locked version 2 schema.
