## What happened

The click-through agent-proposal rehearsal exposed the owner's proposal review page. The owner called the existing page terrible and a first document-first rebuild "AI slop", and pointed back at the 2026-11-16 suggesting-mode prototype as the kind of design they meant. The rebuild had been specified in writing and handed to an implementation worker without that prototype as a visual reference.

Three design rounds followed, all as local mockups under `.workspace/review-routes/` (gitignored), none built into the product:

1. **Four routes.** A quiet Cyberbaser screen; the same screen themed from a handful of deployment tokens; review over the actual website with a private Cyberbaser panel; a quick phone decision card. The owner said Cyberbaser should be "minimal and quiet for the most part and functional where it needs to be", that review could feel like the deployed site if the deployment wants it, and that they liked route C (over the website) most on the phone.
2. **Route C edge cases.** Twelve hard cases: heavy rewrites, whole-note rewrite, 24 scattered fixes, added, removed and moved sections, tables, code, images, list order, invisible changes (link destination, whitespace, hidden comments), properties, a change the owner's rules refuse, stale and expired proposals, long and missing reasons, after-approve, dark site. Findings: past a threshold a block reads as "rewritten" with the old text one tap away; a change with nothing visible must be acknowledged before Approve appears; a refused change shows no Approve. Weak spots: long rewrites strain memory when flipping versions; decisions are all-or-nothing per proposal; route C is the route with real security work, since the decision panel and the page content have different trust levels.
3. **Explainer and prototype.** The owner could not tell what was what, or how the review design relates to the site's design, or whether everything they had asked for was covered. Round three is a labelled three-layer anatomy (your website, the marks, Cyberbaser's panel; the same review on three differently styled sites; what a site can and cannot change) and a tappable phone prototype covering five scenarios and three sender standings, with Approve then a separate Apply. A requirement-by-requirement coverage table was given in the thread. Properties and per-change acceptance are not in the prototype; trust standing and reviewer recommendations appear only as one line of text.

No route is selected. The interim document-first page (commit `adb0d9e`) is kept for its display engine (whole-word change boundaries) and hidden-comment masking, not its look.

## Route C built (owner decision, same day)

Asked "should I build the review experience as prototyped?", the owner chose "Yes, build it as prototyped". Built in the owner app: a review shell on the owner origin with Cyberbaser's bottom panel (desktop: floating side panel), and the note in an `<iframe sandbox="">` served from an owner-only route with `default-src 'none'`, its own `sandbox` CSP directive and `frame-ancestors 'self'`, so vault content can neither run nor reach the decision controls. Previous/next moves the frame by fragment anchors; no script runs in the frame. Heavy edits fold as "Rewritten" (at least 12 words each side and at least 60% of words changed). Changes readers cannot see require "I've seen this", and the server refuses an Approve without `acknowledgedInvisible`; this is the only change to decision handling. Unapplicable changes show no Approve on the page; the server still accepts a direct approval, as before.

**Not yet "as your site shows it".** Every fixture used the fallback: the note rendered from its projection and styled with the site's appearance tokens. Binding the owner-private Quartz build at the proposal's pinned base, with changes placed by source provenance, was not built, because Quartz is not installed in this environment. Also open: hidden-comment changes are acknowledged without being displayed; a stale proposal shows an empty page above the panel; decided proposals show each change in its own sentence because decision records do not keep the page text. Coordinator rerun: owner-alpha, review-projection and proposal-review 807 passed, 8 opt-in skips, 0 failed; browser review tests 2 passed with the acceptance flag.

## The site's own page (path a), same day

When the owner-private Quartz build exists, was built from the proposal's pinned base commit, and its provenance sidecar was rendered from exactly the base bytes (path and SHA-256 checked), the frame now shows that note's built page with each change marked at whole words where its source bytes were rendered. A change spanning several lines is shown line by line under one anchor (display only). Anything that cannot be placed exactly (no provenance, crossing markup, ambiguous, new text the site would render as markup, link destinations, hidden comments, properties) sends the whole page back to the simplified rendering, with one line in Details: "Your site has changed since this was proposed", "your site's page for this note isn't available", or "this change can't be placed on your site's page". The built page is stripped of scripts, handlers, frames and forms, its links are made inert, and its own stylesheets and images are inlined; the frame policy for this page differs from the simplified one only by `img-src data:`. Inlining was chosen over an owner-origin asset route because a `sandbox=""` frame has an opaque origin and cannot carry the owner's `SameSite=Strict` session cookie. Proven on a real pinned Quartz 4.5.2 build of a throwaway vault (opt-in `OWNER_ALPHA_SITE_REVIEW_QUARTZ=1`); not yet used on Cyberbase.

## Rehearsed on real content (owner decision, 2026-11-23)

The owner approved a rehearsal on a read-only local copy of the public vault ("Yes, on a local copy"). Safeguards followed the vault maintainer agent's rules: fresh anonymous single-branch clone of `main` at `2b019b0`, writes only to a throwaway copy whose origin was a local bare repository, all builds inside the contained runner with no network, scratch deleted afterwards.

The first attempt never reached review. The owner-private Quartz build failed twice on real content: source provenance aborted the whole site on one note (HTML indented inside a list item lost its indentation in parsed text but not in the source map), and with provenance off the build tried to fetch Google fonts for social images. Fixed in `2a0609f`: owner builds make no network requests (no social images, local or system fonts, math stylesheet inlined), the public build's resolved configuration is unchanged, the provenance mismatch is fixed, and any future provenance failure drops only that note's map. All 932 notes now build privately offline in about 38 s with provenance.

The second and third runs reached review. Fixed after the second: notes with a frontmatter `slug` (the home page) resolve to their built page; multi-paragraph rewrites are placed on the real page when every block maps exactly; phones open at the first change; after Approve the real page stays while the build matches; decided excerpts use whole words; the simplified view collapses unchanged HTML and runs of hidden content; the job page follows the site's colours and offers Edit again only for the owner's own edits. Result on real notes: a one-phrase change, a three-paragraph rewrite and a home-page link card all show on the real site page; a link-destination change uses the simplified view by design. One proposal was approved and applied to the throwaway copy: one commit, one file, exactly the proposed bytes. Coordinator rerun: 832 passed, 9 opt-in skips, 0 failed.

Still open: the home page loads third-party badge images from its own HTML; a review frame can be about 1 MB; after Apply the decided view falls back to an excerpt once the site is rebuilt; a rewrite that also changes bold or link text in the same paragraph falls back; change anchors such as `c1` could collide with a heading id; on desktop, opening at the change scrolls the title out of view.

## Other findings the same day

- **Socket bug caught by CI on the pinned runtime.** The signed-request socket relied on `listen()` failing when the path already existed. On Bun 1.3.11, the version pinned in the owner-app container and CI, `listen()` replaced an existing regular file instead. The local Bun 1.3.14 masked it. Fixed by refusing any existing entry before binding (`af66287`). The publisher and the intake socket already checked first. Local and CI Bun versions differ; the descriptor defect behind the intermittent `EBADF` test failures is fixed upstream in Bun 1.4.0.
- **Shared frontmatter finder checked on the real vault.** A fresh anonymous single-branch copy of the public vault at `2b019b0` was projected with the tools before the finder change (`main`) and after it (this branch). All 1,308 output files are byte-identical (934 pages, 374 assets) and the reports differ only in the output directory. The copy was deleted afterwards.
- **Delegation lane.** The OpenAI/Codex worker pool ran out on 2026-11-22 (expected back around 2026-11-24). By owner direction workers move to Anthropic models; the round-three explainer and prototype were built in the main session after the guard refused an Opus worker route.
