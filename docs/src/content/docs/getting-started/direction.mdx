**Cyberbaser is in v1 Build: a content-integrity pipeline with governance, now entering routine owner-alpha use.** A default-deny boundary decides what leaves your vault, a commodity renderer turns that into a site, and mechanical validators judge changes: [933 Markdown sources selected and projected into 931 public page URLs](https://cybersader.github.io/cyberbase/), eleven packages including the decision ledger and both WP4 lane adapters, and the GitHub-backed contribution loop rehearsed end to end twice. `OD-01` completed one real reader-to-live owner loop. `OD-02` and `OD-03` are Not run — superseded, with their safety obligations covered mechanically by synthetic `ADV-*` scenarios. The shared proposal contract, Forgejo Lane A adapter, local proposal queue, account-free Lane B package/app, disabled Quartz form, and local-only sibling-service bundle are implemented and mechanically tested at their stated layers. Lane A is not live-validated, installed, linked, or offered. Lane B is off by default, unexposed, human-untested, and no source writer or production authority. Q09 remains open. The five-reader protocol is preserved for later only if stronger independent usability evidence is needed.

This page exists to answer one question in under two minutes: **is this still your project?** Read the scorecard, then the forks. Everything below those two is depth you can skip.

:::note[How this page is maintained]
This is the alignment instrument, so it gets **updated, never replaced**.

1. **Every claim cites something**: an R-number from the decision log, a measured number, or a file. No adjectives standing in for evidence.
2. **A divergence leaves this page only when a decision closes it**, never because it got uncomfortable. That rule is [R20](/cyberbaser/getting-started/roadmap/#reopened-research-deleted-in-error-2026-07-25-restored-2026-07-26), written after four open questions were deleted in a tidy-up commit.
3. **When a fork is decided**, it moves from "Yours to decide" into the decision story with its R-number, and the rejected option stays visible so the trade is still readable a year later.
4. **Update in the same session as the decision**, alongside `.claude/41-QUESTIONS-RESOLVED.md` and `FOCUS.md`. A stale alignment page is worse than none.
:::

The contribution gaps are the same gap wearing different clothes: Cyberbaser has built **judgement** (boundaries, validators, a trust classifier) and now the local mechanics of an account-free **surface**, but has not shipped or human-validated that surface. The disabled renderer form, strict sibling intake service, retained exact-source resolver, and local queue stop at `pending-review`; they do not decide or write source. `OD-01` tested the real boundary once. The private owner-alpha wiki now implements the automatic exact source-to-live path, passed a hermetic browser plus restart-recovery rehearsal, and completed its first real policy-activated acceptance Save on 2026-08-02 (verified commit, normal push, successful deployment, confirmed live transition). Routine use now exposes operational friction to fix. The deferred concierge pilot remains available if Cyberbaser later needs unfamiliar-reader or independent-owner usability evidence. Federation is separate: its authority model survived a bounded local fixture, while independent interoperability and the live URL contract remain unproven.

## How we got here

Twenty-two decisions, as a story rather than a log. One line each: what we decided, and why.

### Act 1: what is this thing?

- **R02** Cyberbaser is *an interoperability layer for contributable, version-controlled knowledge bases*, because "publishing tool" is a category Quartz already wins and "CMS" throws away the interoperability half.
- **R06** The hub is the product and stays renderer-agnostic, because forward publishing is commoditised and the reverse direction is structurally impossible inside any SSG, so it is genuinely unclaimed.
- **R07** The docs site is the canonical knowledge base and `.claude/` is a pointer layer, because the two had drifted into disagreeing brains and agents read the stale one first.
- **R08** No external demand gate before building, because you are user #1 and will use it either way.

<details>
<summary>The reasoning, and what each one cost</summary>

**R02** came out of a 12-agent essence panel plus your own choices. The consequence that still binds: every page leads with the essence, and "Obsidian Publish successor" stays a private framing. Your "Wikipedia replacement / modern wiki / web of webs" language is sharper than the locked one-liner and is not yet reconciled with it. That is a proposal on the [vision page](/cyberbaser/getting-started/vision/), not a unilateral rewrite.

**R06** was decided by auditing Quartz: it beat our own prototype on Obsidian-syntax fidelity for free, and its maintainer had publicly closed CMS and web-editor requests as out of scope. That is the cleanest possible signal about which half of the problem is worth owning.

**R07** converted twelve numbered files into stubs. The rule it created (propagate a locked decision to the orientation layer in the same session) is the reason this page has a maintenance contract at the top.

**R08** removed a gate, which was correct for a dogfood-first tool, but it also removed the one mechanism that would have surfaced "does anyone else want this". The [operational-model question](/cyberbaser/agent-context/zz-challenges/01-identity-operational-model/) is where that comes back.

</details>

### Act 2: what can it stand on?

- **R01** Git stays the source of truth, scoped as the current manifestation with an off-ramp, because 83+ alternatives were surveyed and none fixes git's two structural zeros without breaking the plain-`.md` constraint.
- **R05** The round-trip is achievable, because a one-afternoon spike round-tripped 20 of 21 fixtures.
- **R12** No whole-file parse/stringify regeneration in any Cyberbaser-mediated source update, because the same pipeline over all 1,430 real vault files returned **4.6% byte identity**. The serializer-backed writer is architecturally incapable of preserving untouched bytes, so `@cyberbaser/ofm` became a validator. Rich editor or CMS-shaped presentation remains allowed through an exact-proposal adapter.
- **R13** The Notion sync is frozen and absorbed, because the audit found it last ran on 2024-08-20. The "live third writer" that three analyses worried about did not exist.

<details>
<summary>The reasoning, and what each one cost</summary>

**R01's** two structural zeros are sub-repo access control and federation. Both are v2 pillars. R10 later assigned access control to a sibling project and left federation with nobody, which is exactly why federation shows up as a gap on the scorecard above.

**R05 was over-claimed and R12 corrected it.** The spike proved that masking protects Obsidian constructs *through a parse*, which is true and still stands. It did not prove that real files round-trip, and 20 of 21 turned out to be a property of fixtures written in the serializer's own output style. This is the healthiest thing in the log: a favourite result was falsified by its own follow-up measurement rather than defended.

**R12 is the load-bearing decision of the whole project.** It killed a planned workstream, re-scoped the flagship package, and is the reason the codebase is roughly 2,200 lines instead of 20,000. It also means the moat is byte-preservation plus mechanical validation plus governance, not translation.

**R13** left one loose end worth remembering: the repo description still advertises a sync that has been dead for two years.

</details>

### Act 3: who is it for, and who else does the work?

- **R03** Contribution is a maintainer-set trust curve plus a moderation queue, never a forced account, because identity gates are mostly theatre (email aliases are free) and moderation is the mechanism that actually carries the weight.
- **R10** Mixed privacy, RBAC and real-time collaboration belong to a private sibling project, because it already ships them and rebuilding a running system is not a good use of a solo maintainer.
- **R11** No secrets scan and no history purge, because the public vault content is deliberate. Selective publishing survives as a feature, not an emergency.
- **R19** The 669 remaining broken links and the 43 emoji folders stay, because they are inherited content debt and your daily Obsidian navigation.

<details>
<summary>The reasoning, and what each one cost</summary>

**R03's** real content is the question it replaces. Not "who are you" but "can this hurt anything before a human approves it". Denial of service is a separate, edge-layer problem, which is why an account wall is not the answer to it.

**R10** is a good decision recorded with a broken reason. The [vision page](/cyberbaser/getting-started/vision/) still gates real-time collaboration on "the round-trip keystone", and R12 dissolved that keystone, so the stated re-entry condition can never be met. The decision should be re-stated as "owned elsewhere", not "waiting".

**R11** removed a triage step and reworded the Phase 3 gate from safety blocker to feature prerequisite. The publish boundary that shipped later is the feature that requirement asked for.

**R19** turned the link check into a **ratchet**: the baseline is frozen at the measured number, pre-existing debt stays silent, and any regression cyberbaser introduces fails. That is the pattern to copy for every future quality gate.

</details>

### Act 4: ship it

- **R09** Decide the v1 shape by measurement with the rule fixed in advance, because a four-critic consensus had already been overturned once by a dissent pass, and locking on argument would have repeated the documented mistake.
- **R14** Quartz renders the vault and Starlight keeps these docs, because the real vault built in 1:55 and scored 20 of 20 on the Obsidian-syntax checklist, and the path census found 0 slug collisions, which killed the mass-rename plan.
- **R15** Publish grants are read only from the `cb-publish:` key, because the first dry run found **587 stale `publish: true` flags**, including on daily notes and personal finance files.
- **R16** The vault is live, with 933 Markdown sources selected and projected into 931 public page URLs, because the boundary, projection and leak test all held on the first real run: 160 s build, 16 s deploy, 25% of the Pages size cap.
- **R17** The contribution loop is closed and proven, because two rehearsal PRs ran the full circuit, and rehearsal #1 caught a crash that report-only mode had been hiding behind a green check.
- **R18** Site quality and the trust curve shipped on measurement: 1,145 broken links down to 779, the attachment class 248 down to 7, and a trust classifier that fails closed.

<details>
<summary>The reasoning, and what each one cost</summary>

**R09 is a process decision worth keeping.** State the decision rule before the measurement, then let the number decide. Both branches of the rule turned out to rest on a false premise, and finding that out was more valuable than either branch.

**R14** also settled the filename question that had been open for months: 4,183 paths, 0 collisions, 2 lint violations. The mass rename that several plans assumed is dead.

**R15** carries a second, quieter lock: configs live in YAML but you never hand-author them. Agents draft, you approve effects (dry-run tables, not syntax). That makes the config *experience* a product surface, which is one of the few places a cyberbaser UI is already justified.

**R16** shipped two known defects on purpose and wrote them down: a 28-page relative-link class, and Quartz's silent 5.7% unresolved-link rate. R18 then fixed the first with a 47-line renderer plugin.

**R17's** lesson generalises past this project: report-only mode hides crashes as well as verdicts, so an observation window has to include reading the logs, not just the checkmarks.

**R18** left the honest reading in the record: 1,145 broken links is 1.36% site-wide but 51.5% of the links inside note bodies, because roughly 98% of the denominator is navigation chrome that never breaks.

</details>

### Act 5: tell the truth about it

- **R20** An open question may be re-pointed, re-worded or re-filed, but never deleted, because four research tasks were removed in a housekeeping commit with no decision, including one you added yourself by name.
- **R21** The knowledge base was audited against the code and repaired, and the answer to "is this just a public tech stack workflow?" is: two things are genuinely ours (the OFM change classifier and the publish boundary), and the rest is careful assembly. No hosted console for the dogfood case.
- **R22** You must not need GitHub either. Recorded the day you said it, and it re-opens R21's console answer, because "the forge is the UI" only holds for a maintainer who has a forge.

<details>
<summary>The reasoning, and what each one cost</summary>

**R20** is the uncomfortable one. The deleted task was titled *"operational model and sustainability"*, added in your own commit, and its text ("which layer creates enough value that someone would pay… 'what is cyberbaser' and 'what's the business' answer together") is a paraphrase of the question you asked eleven weeks later that triggered this whole alignment pass. The question came back because nothing answered it. All four tasks are restored on the [roadmap](/cyberbaser/getting-started/roadmap/#reopened-research-deleted-in-error-2026-07-25-restored-2026-07-26).

**R21 priced the console honestly.** A hosted console costs about 13.5 maintainer-days plus roughly 1 day a month forever, against a *measured* capacity of about 4.7 maintainer-days a month (96 commits across 11 distinct calendar days in 219 days). That is 21% of everything you have, permanently. The alternative it identified: every non-realtime promise is satisfiable by static generation plus CI annotation for 4.5 to 6.75 days with no runtime at all, because the data already exists as JSON that no human has ever seen rendered.

R21 also corrected its own earlier claim. "151 tests green" was partly hollow, since about 40% of one package's suite covered code nothing called. Wiring that code revealed something worse: the projection's collision guard was a no-op under verbatim paths, so the pipeline had no collision detection at all. Now wired, 165 tests, and the workflow's path-lint claim became true instead of needing a walk-back.

**R22** is a scope-defining decision, and it belongs to you rather than to another research pass. It connects straight back to R20: the deleted task listed candidate value centres including, verbatim, *hosted service ("bring your vault, get a contributable wiki")*, which is one of the three answers on the table in Fork 1.

</details>
